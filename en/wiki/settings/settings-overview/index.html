Settings Overview

Chapter 14: Settings Overview

Introduction

Welcome to the control center of SeaMeet! If the main interface is like the cockpit where you fly the plane, the Settings menu is like the maintenance bay where you fine-tune the engine, adjust the seats, and customize your flight experience. This chapter will show you how to access the Settings menu and give you a roadmap of all the different categories you'll find there.

By the end of this chapter, you'll know exactly where to find every setting in SeaMeet and understand what each category controls. Think of this as your orientation tour before we dive deep into each specific section.

Chapter Objectives

After reading this chapter, you will be able to:

  • Open the Settings menu from multiple locations in SeaMeet
  • Understand the 8 main settings categories and what they control
  • Navigate between different settings sections quickly
  • Recognize the visual indicators and icons used in the settings interface
  • Know when to change which settings based on your needs

What Are Settings, and Why Do They Matter?

Think of SeaMeet settings like the preferences on your smartphone. You don't need to change them to use the basic features, but customizing them makes the app work exactly how YOU want it to work. It's the difference between wearing a one-size-fits-all shirt versus one tailored to your measurements.

Settings matter because they allow you to:

Personalize Your Experience

  • Choose your preferred language (16 options available!)
  • Set light or dark mode based on your eyes' comfort
  • Pick where your recordings are saved
  • Configure shortcuts that match your muscle memory

Optimize Performance

  • Adjust audio quality based on your microphone capabilities
  • Set video resolution to match your screen and storage needs
  • Enable or disable features based on your computer's power
  • Manage memory usage for flashback recording

Ensure Reliability

  • Set up auto-detection so you never miss a meeting
  • Configure storage warnings before you run out of space
  • Choose the right audio devices for your setup
  • Set up permissions correctly from the start

Solve Problems Before They Happen

  • Adjust microphone gain if people say you're too quiet or loud
  • Set default recording modes to avoid forgetting to switch
  • Configure auto-start options so recording begins when you want it
  • Enable low-memory mode if your computer is older or slower

How to Open the Settings Menu

SeaMeet gives you multiple ways to access Settings, just like how you can unlock your phone with a password, fingerprint, or face recognition. Here are all the ways to get to your settings:

Method 1: From the Left Sidebar

This is the most common way to access Settings. Look at the vertical sidebar on the left side of the main SeaMeet window.

Step-by-Step:

  1. Find the left sidebar with icons for Recordings, Video, Flashback, etc.

  2. Look for the Settings icon — it looks like a gear or cogwheel (⚙️)

  3. Click on the Settings icon

  4. The main area will switch from showing your recordings to showing the Settings panel

Visual Reference:

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  [🎤] [🎥] [📼] [🔍] [⚙️ Settings]                         │
│   Rec   Vid   Flash  Auto    ← Settings icon here!          │
│                                                             │
│  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐  │
│  │                                                      │  │
│  │        SETTINGS PANEL OPENS HERE                     │  │
│  │                                                      │  │
│  └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘  │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Method 2: From the Menu Bar (Windows)

If you're on Windows, you can also access Settings through the traditional menu bar at the top of the window.

Step-by-Step:

  1. Click on "File" in the top menu bar

  2. Look for "Settings" or "Preferences" in the dropdown menu

  3. Click on it to open the Settings panel

Note: This method may vary slightly depending on your Windows version and SeaMeet version.

Method 3: Keyboard Shortcut (Windows)

For the keyboard enthusiasts out there, there's a quick shortcut:

On Windows: Press Ctrl + Comma (,)

This is a common shortcut across many Windows applications (including Microsoft Office and many others), so it might already be in your muscle memory.

How to Use:

  1. Make sure SeaMeet is the active window (click on it if you're not sure)
  2. Hold down Ctrl
  3. While holding Ctrl, press the comma key
  4. Settings will open immediately

Method 4: From the Tray Menu (Windows)

SeaMeet runs in your system tray (that's the area near your clock with all those little icons). You can access Settings from there too.

Step-by-Step:

  1. Look for the SeaMeet icon in your system tray (bottom-right corner of screen)

    • You might need to click the little upward arrow to see hidden icons

  2. Right-click on the SeaMeet icon

  3. A context menu will appear with various options

  4. Click on "Settings" from the menu

Why Use This Method?

  • Great when SeaMeet is minimized
  • Quick access without opening the main window
  • Perfect when you're in a hurry

Method 5: From the Floater Menu

Remember the floating control widget (the Floater) from Chapter 12? You can access Settings from there too.

Step-by-Step:

  1. Click on the Floater to expand it (if it's in compact mode)

  2. Look for a settings gear icon or menu option

  3. Click on it to open Settings

Note: The exact location may vary based on your Floater configuration, but the settings option is always accessible from the expanded Floater view.

The 8 Settings Categories Explained

SeaMeet organizes settings into 8 logical categories. Think of these like drawers in a filing cabinet — each one contains related items:

Category 1: General Settings

Icon: Usually a gear or house icon What It Controls: Basic app behavior and appearance

Typical Settings Include:

  • Startup behavior — Should SeaMeet open automatically when your computer starts?
  • Minimize to tray — Should SeaMeet keep running when you close the window?
  • Theme selection — Light mode, dark mode, or automatic (follows system)
  • Notification preferences — Which events should trigger notifications?
  • Update settings — Should SeaMeet check for updates automatically?

When to Use This Category:

  • When you first install SeaMeet and want to set up basic preferences
  • When you want to change the app's look and feel
  • When you want to control how SeaMeet behaves in the background
  • When setting up automatic startup

Analogy: This is like adjusting your car's basic settings — should the lights turn on automatically? Should it remember your seat position? Should it ding when you leave the keys inside?

Category 2: Storage Settings

Icon: Usually a hard drive or folder icon What It Controls: Where and how your recordings are saved

Typical Settings Include:

  • Save location — Which folder should recordings go into?
  • Storage management — Automatic cleanup of old recordings
  • File organization — How should files be named and sorted?
  • Disk space warnings — Alert when you're running low on space
  • Backup settings — Should recordings be backed up to cloud storage?

When to Use This Category:

  • When you want to change where recordings are saved (like moving them to an external drive)
  • When you're running low on disk space and need to manage storage
  • When you want to organize recordings in a specific way
  • When setting up automatic cleanup to prevent accumulation

Analogy: This is like organizing your closet — deciding which drawer gets which clothes, how to label boxes, and when to donate old items to make room for new ones.

Important Note: This is covered in detail in Chapter 15 (Storage Settings), where we'll walk through every option step-by-step.

Category 3: Audio Settings

Icon: Usually a microphone or speaker icon What It Controls: Everything related to sound recording and playback

Typical Settings Include:

  • Default microphone — Which microphone to use for recording
  • Microphone gain — How loud your voice should be recorded
  • System audio source — Which audio output to capture
  • Bluetooth warnings — Alerts when using Bluetooth microphones
  • Audio format — Preferred format for audio recordings (MP3, WAV, etc.)
  • Sample rate — Audio quality level (44.1kHz, 48kHz, etc.)

When to Use This Category:

  • When people say you're too quiet or too loud in recordings
  • When you have multiple microphones and want to choose a default
  • When using Bluetooth headphones and need latency warnings
  • When you want to change audio quality or file formats
  • When troubleshooting audio recording issues

Analogy: This is like adjusting the sound system in a concert hall — choosing which microphones to use, setting the right volume levels, and ensuring the acoustics work perfectly for the performance.

Important Note: This is covered in detail in Chapter 16 (Audio Settings), where we'll dive deep into microphone selection, gain control, and more.

Category 4: Video Settings

Icon: Usually a video camera or monitor icon What It Controls: Everything related to screen recording

Typical Settings Include:

  • Default recording mode — Fullscreen, window, or region
  • Video quality — Resolution (1080p, 720p, 480p)
  • Frame rate — How smooth the video should be (30fps, 60fps)
  • Monitor selection — Which screen to record (if you have multiple)
  • Video format — Preferred format for video files (MP4, WebM, etc.)
  • Hardware acceleration — Use GPU for better performance
  • Cursor recording — Should the mouse pointer be visible?
  • Cursor effects — Highlight clicks, change cursor size, etc.

When to Use This Category:

  • When you want to set a default recording mode to save clicks
  • When you need to balance quality vs. file size
  • When you have multiple monitors and need to specify which to record
  • When recording gameplay and need high frame rates (60fps)
  • When creating tutorials and want cursor effects

Analogy: This is like setting up a movie camera — choosing the film quality, deciding which lens to use, setting the frame rate for smooth action, and deciding whether to show the cameraman's hand in the shot.

Important Note: This is covered in detail in Chapter 17 (Video Settings), where we'll explain all the technical terms and help you choose the right settings.

Category 5: Flashback Settings

Icon: Usually a circular arrow or clock icon (representing "rewind") What It Controls: The time-machine recording feature

Typical Settings Include:

  • Auto-start Flashback — Should Flashback start automatically when SeaMeet launches?
  • Buffer duration — How far back in time to keep (10-120 seconds)
  • Audio buffer length — How much audio to keep (fixed at 30 seconds)
  • Video quality — Resolution for Flashback video (often lower than regular recording)
  • Low-memory mode — Reduce memory usage for older computers
  • Buffer persistence — Keep buffer when switching apps?

When to Use This Category:

  • When you want Flashback to always be ready (auto-start)
  • When you need to balance how far back you can save vs. memory usage
  • When your computer is older and you need to conserve RAM
  • When you want to customize how Flashback behaves

Analogy: This is like configuring a security camera's DVR — deciding how many days of footage to keep, setting the recording quality, and managing storage space for the constant recording.

Important Note: This is covered in detail in Chapter 18 (Flashback Settings), where we'll help you find the perfect balance between buffer length and performance.

Category 6: Auto-Recording Settings

Icon: Usually a magnifying glass or robot/automation icon What It Controls: Automatic meeting detection and recording

Typical Settings Include:

  • Auto-detection enabled — Master on/off switch for the feature
  • Detection sensitivity — How aggressive the detection should be
  • Default action — Start recording immediately or just notify?
  • Per-app permissions — Which apps can trigger auto-recording
  • Detection methods — Which signals to watch for (window title, audio, etc.)
  • Delay before start — Wait a few seconds after detection to begin
  • Post-meeting actions — What to do when a meeting ends

When to Use This Category:

  • When setting up SeaMeet for the first time and enabling auto-detection
  • When you want to fine-tune which apps trigger recording
  • When you find auto-detection too sensitive (or not sensitive enough)
  • When you want SeaMeet to wait a moment before starting
  • When customizing what happens after meetings end

Analogy: This is like training a smart assistant — teaching it to recognize when you're in a meeting, deciding which meetings matter, and telling it how to respond when it detects one.

Important Note: This is covered in detail in Chapter 19 (Auto-Recording Settings), where we'll walk through setting up permissions for all 47+ supported apps.

When to Use This Category:

  • When you want to use SeaMeet in your native language
  • When date/time formats don't match your region's conventions
  • When working across time zones and needing accurate timestamps
  • When the default language isn't your preference

Analogy: This is like setting up a GPS navigation system — choosing your preferred language for voice directions, selecting miles vs kilometers, and setting your home time zone.

Category 8: About SeaMeet

Understanding Setting Controls

Different settings use different types of controls. Here's a guide to recognizing and using each type:

Toggle Switches (On/Off)

What They Look Like: A horizontal switch that slides left (off) or right (on). Often colored when on (usually blue or green).

Setting Name          [━━●──]   ← Off position
                      [──●━━]   ← On position (colored)

How to Use:

  • Click on the switch to toggle it
  • The switch will slide and change color
  • The change usually takes effect immediately

Examples in SeaMeet:

  • Auto-start Flashback
  • Minimize to system tray
  • Enable auto-detection
  • Show cursor in recordings

Analogy: Like a light switch on your wall — flip it one way for on, the other for off.

Dropdown Menus

What They Look Like: A rectangular field showing the current selection with a downward arrow on the right.

Recording Quality     [1080p HD        ▼]
                       ↑ Current      ↑ Click to see
                         value          all options

How to Use:

  1. Click on the dropdown (or the arrow)
  2. A list of options appears below
  3. Click on your desired option
  4. The dropdown closes with your new selection showing

Examples in SeaMeet:

  • Video quality (1080p, 720p, 480p)
  • Frame rate (30fps, 60fps)
  • Audio format (MP3, WAV, WebM)
  • Default recording mode (Fullscreen, Window, Region)
  • Display language (16 options)

Analogy: Like a restaurant menu — you see the current selection on your plate, but you can open the menu to choose something different.

Text Input Fields

What They Look Like: An empty or pre-filled rectangular box where you can type.

Save Location        [C:\Users\You\Recordings    ] [Browse...]
                       ↑ Click here to type      ↑ Or click to
                                                   browse folders

How to Use:

  1. Click inside the text field
  2. Type your text (or clear existing text first)
  3. Press Tab or click elsewhere when done

Special Features:

  • Browse button — Often next to path fields, opens a file browser
  • Placeholder text — Gray text showing example input
  • Validation — May turn red if you enter something invalid

Examples in SeaMeet:

  • Save directory path
  • File naming pattern
  • Custom keyboard shortcuts
  • Microphone device name (sometimes)

Analogy: Like filling out a form at the doctor's office — you write information in blank fields.

Sliders

What They Look Like: A horizontal bar with a handle you can drag left or right.

Microphone Gain      [━●━━━━━━━━━━━] 45%
                       ↑ Drag this knob
                         left (quieter)
                         or right (louder)

How to Use:

  1. Click and hold the slider handle (or knob)
  2. Drag it left to decrease the value
  3. Drag it right to increase the value
  4. Release when satisfied

Alternative: Click anywhere on the slider bar to jump to that position

Examples in SeaMeet:

  • Microphone gain/volume
  • Detection sensitivity (for auto-recording)
  • Buffer duration (for Flashback)
  • Quality/compression balance

Analogy: Like a volume knob on a stereo — turn left for quieter, right for louder.

Buttons

What They Look Like: Rectangular or rounded buttons with text labels.

[Check for Updates] [Reset to Defaults] [Browse...]

How to Use:

  • Simply click the button once
  • Something happens immediately (dialog opens, action performed, etc.)

Types of Buttons:

  • Action buttons — Perform an action (Check for Updates, Browse)
  • Reset buttons — Restore defaults (Reset to Defaults)
  • Navigation buttons — Go to other screens (Advanced Settings)
  • Confirmation buttons — Save or cancel changes (Apply, OK, Cancel)

Examples in SeaMeet:

  • Browse (for choosing folders)
  • Reset to Defaults (for any category)
  • Check for Updates (in About section)
  • Apply/Save changes
  • Test microphone (in Audio settings)

Analogy: Like elevator buttons — press once and the elevator does something (go up, go down, open doors).

Checkboxes

What They Look Like: A small square that can be checked (✓) or empty.

☐ Start SeaMeet when computer starts
☑ Minimize to system tray on close
☐ Show recording notifications

How to Use:

  • Click on the checkbox to toggle it
  • A checkmark appears when enabled
  • Click again to uncheck

Difference from Toggles:

  • Checkboxes are typically used when you can select multiple options
  • Toggles are used for single on/off choices
  • SeaMeet primarily uses toggles, but you might see checkboxes in advanced settings

Analogy: Like a to-do list — you check off items you've completed.

Settings Behavior: Immediate vs. Applied

Not all settings work the same way when you change them. There are two main behaviors:

Immediate Settings

These take effect as soon as you change them — no need to click "Apply" or "Save."

Examples:

  • Theme changes (light/dark mode)
  • Language selection
  • Toggle switches (most of them)
  • Volume/gain sliders (you hear the change in real-time)

How to Recognize Them:

  • Changes happen instantly
  • No "Apply" button needed
  • Usually visual changes or simple toggles

Applied Settings

These require you to click "Apply," "OK," or "Save" before they take effect.

Examples:

  • Save location path
  • Default recording mode
  • Complex audio/video configurations
  • Some permission settings

How to Recognize Them:

  • An "Apply" or "Save" button is present
  • You might see "unsaved changes" indicators
  • Changes don't happen until you confirm

Important: If you change settings and click "Cancel," your changes will be discarded! Always click "Apply" or "OK" if you want to keep your changes.

The Search Feature (If Available)

Some versions of SeaMeet include a search box at the top of the Settings panel. This is incredibly useful!

How to Use Search:

  1. Look for a search box (usually says "Search settings..." or has a magnifying glass icon)

  2. Click in the search box and type keywords:

    • "microphone" → Shows all microphone-related settings
    • "save" → Shows storage and save location settings
    • "quality" → Shows video and audio quality settings
    • "shortcut" → Shows keyboard shortcut settings

  3. As you type, the settings list filters to show only matching items

  4. Click on a result to jump directly to that setting

Why Search Is Helpful:

  • Find settings quickly without browsing categories
  • Discover related settings you didn't know existed
  • Great when you're not sure which category a setting is in
  • Saves time when you know exactly what you want to change

Common Settings Workflows

Here are some typical scenarios and the exact steps to handle them:

Scenario 1: First-Time Setup

You're new to SeaMeet and want to configure the basics:

  1. Open Settings (click the gear icon in the left sidebar)

  2. Go to General

    • Set theme to your preference
    • Enable "Minimize to tray" if desired
    • Enable "Start with computer" if desired

  3. Go to Storage

    • Verify or change the save location
    • Set up disk space warnings

  4. Go to Audio

    • Select your default microphone
    • Test the microphone and adjust gain
    • Check Bluetooth warnings if using wireless headphones

  5. Go to Language

    • Select your preferred display language

  6. Click Apply to save all changes

  7. Go to About to note your version number (handy for support)

Scenario 2: Changing Where Recordings Are Saved

Your C: drive is filling up and you want to move recordings to an external drive:

  1. Open Settings

  2. Click on Storage in the left sidebar

  3. Find "Save Location" or "Recording Directory"

  4. Click the "Browse" button

  5. Navigate to your external drive in the file browser

    • Example: D:\SeaMeet Recordings

  6. Select or create a folder

  7. Click OK in the file browser

  8. Click Apply in Settings

  9. Optional: Move your existing recordings to the new location using File Explorer

Scenario 3: Setting Up Auto-Detection

You want SeaMeet to automatically detect Zoom meetings:

  1. Open Settings

  2. Click on Auto-Recording

  3. Enable "Auto-Detection" (toggle switch)

  4. Find Zoom in the apps list

  5. Enable permission for Zoom (toggle or checkbox)

  6. Choose default action: Start recording immediately or just notify?

  7. Adjust sensitivity if needed (slider)

  8. Click Apply

  9. Test it: Open Zoom and start a meeting to see if SeaMeet detects it

Scenario 4: Optimizing for an Older Computer

Your computer is a few years old and running slowly during recordings:

  1. Open Settings

  2. Go to Video

    • Lower video quality to 720p or 480p
    • Set frame rate to 30fps (instead of 60fps)
    • Disable hardware acceleration if it's causing issues

  3. Go to Flashback

    • Enable "Low-memory mode"
    • Reduce buffer duration to 30-60 seconds (instead of 120)

  4. Go to Audio

    • Choose a lower sample rate if available (44.1kHz instead of 48kHz)

  5. Go to Storage

    • Enable automatic cleanup to prevent disk space issues

  6. Click Apply

  7. Test a recording to see if performance improves

Scenario 5: Preparing for a Professional Presentation

You need to record a high-quality presentation for work:

  1. Open Settings

  2. Go to Video

    • Set quality to 1080p (or highest available)
    • Set frame rate to 60fps for smooth motion
    • Enable "Record cursor"
    • Enable "Highlight cursor clicks" if available

  3. Go to Audio

    • Select your best microphone
    • Set gain to optimal level (test first!)
    • Choose high-quality format (WAV for best, or high-bitrate MP3)

  4. Go to General

    • Disable notifications to prevent interruptions
    • Ensure "Minimize to tray" is enabled

  5. Go to Storage

    • Verify you have enough disk space (HD video uses lots of space!)

  6. Click Apply

  7. Do a 30-second test recording to verify everything looks and sounds perfect

Settings Tips and Tricks

Tip 1: The "Reset to Defaults" Safety Net

If you change settings and something stops working, don't panic! Every category has a "Reset to Defaults" button that returns that section to factory settings.

How to Use:

  1. Go to the problematic category
  2. Click "Reset to Defaults" (usually at the bottom)
  3. Confirm the reset
  4. Try again with clean settings

Caution: This resets ALL settings in that category, not just the one you changed last.

Tip 2: Export/Import Settings (If Available)

Some versions of SeaMeet let you export your settings to a file. This is great for:

  • Backing up your preferences
  • Transferring settings to another computer
  • Sharing optimized settings with colleagues

Look for: "Export Settings" or "Import Settings" buttons, usually in General or at the bottom of the Settings panel.

Tip 3: Keyboard Navigation

You can navigate settings without a mouse:

  • Tab — Move to the next control
  • Shift + Tab — Move to the previous control
  • Spacebar — Toggle checkboxes or activate buttons
  • Arrow keys — Navigate dropdown options or adjust sliders
  • Enter — Confirm selections or activate buttons

This is especially useful for accessibility or when your mouse isn't working.

Tip 4: Hover for Help

Many settings have helpful tooltips. Hover your mouse over a setting name or control to see additional information.

Example: Hover over "Microphone Gain" might show: "Adjust this if your recordings are too quiet or too loud. 50% is the default."

Tip 5: Settings Are Saved Automatically (Usually)

In most cases, SeaMeet saves your settings automatically when you change them. However, some complex changes require clicking "Apply."

Signs that you need to click Apply:

  • An "Apply" button is visible and clickable
  • You see text like "Unsaved changes"
  • The setting involves file paths or external resources

When in doubt: Click Apply! It never hurts to confirm your changes.

Understanding Settings Persistence

Here's something important to understand: Settings are saved to your user profile and persist between sessions.

What This Means:

  • Settings survive when you close and reopen SeaMeet
  • Settings stay the same even if you restart your computer
  • Settings are tied to your Windows/Mac user account
  • If you have multiple user accounts, each has its own settings

Where Settings Are Stored (Technical):

  • Windows: Usually in your user profile folder, often in AppData
  • macOS: In your user Library folder
  • You don't need to access these files directly — use the Settings UI

Exceptions:

  • Some portable versions might store settings in the app folder
  • Enterprise deployments might use centralized settings
  • Reinstalling SeaMeet usually preserves user settings

Settings Security and Privacy

SeaMeet settings are local to your computer and generally don't contain sensitive information. However:

What's in Settings:

  • File paths (may reveal folder structure)
  • App preferences (which features you use)
  • Hardware choices (which microphone you prefer)
  • No passwords or account credentials

Privacy Considerations:

  • Settings don't include your actual recordings (just where they're saved)
  • Auto-detection settings show which apps you use (but not usage data)
  • No cloud syncing of settings (everything stays local)

Corporate Environments:

  • IT administrators might be able to view or manage settings
  • Group policy might override some settings
  • Check with your IT department if you have concerns

Troubleshooting Settings Issues

Problem 1: Settings Won't Save

Symptoms: You change a setting, but it reverts back when you reopen Settings.

Solutions:

  1. Make sure you click "Apply" — Some settings require explicit saving
  2. Check disk space — If your drive is full, settings can't be saved
  3. Run as administrator — Try right-clicking SeaMeet and "Run as administrator"
  4. Check permissions — Ensure you have write access to your user profile folder
  5. Reinstall SeaMeet — Backup your recordings first, then reinstall

Problem 2: Settings Are Grayed Out

Symptoms: Some settings are visible but can't be changed (grayed out or disabled).

Common Causes:

  • Another setting disables them — For example, you can't set video quality if video recording is disabled
  • Admin/enterprise restrictions — Your organization might lock certain settings
  • Feature not available — That feature might not be included in your version
  • Hardware limitations — Some settings require specific hardware

Solutions:

  1. Check if you need to enable a parent setting first
  2. Contact your IT department if it's an enterprise restriction
  3. Check SeaMeet version (some features are version-specific)
  4. Verify your hardware supports the feature

Problem 3: Settings UI Is Glitched

Symptoms: Settings look wrong, overlap, or don't display properly.

Solutions:

  1. Resize the window — Try making the SeaMeet window larger
  2. Restart SeaMeet — Close and reopen the app
  3. Update graphics drivers — Outdated drivers can cause UI issues
  4. Change display scaling — If using high DPI (4K), try 100% scaling temporarily
  5. Reinstall SeaMeet — If all else fails, a fresh install usually fixes UI issues

Problem 4: Can't Find a Setting

Symptoms: You know a setting exists, but you can't locate it.

Solutions:

  1. Use search — Type keywords in the settings search box
  2. Check all categories — It might be in an unexpected category
  3. Check advanced settings — Some settings are hidden behind an "Advanced" button
  4. Update SeaMeet — The setting might be in a newer version
  5. Read the manual — Each settings chapter lists all available options

Advanced: Settings File Location (For Power Users)

If you're a technical user and need to access settings directly, here's where to find them:

Windows

Standard Installation:

C:\Users\[YourUsername]\AppData\Roaming\SeaMeet\

File names might include:

  • settings.json
  • config.json
  • preferences.json
  • SeaMeet.config

How to Access:

  1. Press Windows + R
  2. Type %AppData%\SeaMeet and press Enter
  3. Look for settings files

Warning: Editing these files manually can corrupt SeaMeet. Always backup first!

macOS

Standard Location:

~/Library/Application Support/SeaMeet/

Or in preferences:

~/Library/Preferences/com.seameet.app.plist

How to Access:

  1. Open Finder
  2. Press Command + Shift + G
  3. Type ~/Library/Application Support/SeaMeet
  4. Click Go

When to Edit Settings Files Directly

Generally, you should never need to do this, but it might be helpful for:

  • Migrating settings between computers without the cloud
  • Bulk editing multiple settings at once
  • Recovering settings from a backup
  • Troubleshooting when the UI won't open

Always:

  • Close SeaMeet completely before editing
  • Make a backup copy first
  • Use a proper JSON/text editor (Notepad++, VS Code, etc.)
  • Verify the file format is correct before saving

Settings Glossary: Quick Reference

Here's a quick glossary of terms you'll see throughout Settings:

Enable/Disable — Turn a feature on or off Default — The setting used unless you specify otherwise Path/Directory — A location on your computer (like C:\Users\You\Documents) Browse — Open a file browser to select a location Bitrate — How much data is used per second (higher = better quality, larger files) Sample Rate — How often audio is captured per second (44.1kHz = 44,100 times/second) Resolution — Video image size (1080p = 1920×1080 pixels) Frame Rate — How many video frames per second (fps) Buffer — Temporary memory storage for ongoing data Gain — Volume level of input (microphone sensitivity) Codec — Software that compresses/decompresses audio/video Format — File type (MP3, MP4, WebM, etc.) Toggle — A switch that turns something on or off Apply — Save the changes you've made Reset — Return to original/default values
